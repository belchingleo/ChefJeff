## 0.9.0-beta.1 — 2026-11-11 · sixth public test release

### Step hints on by default with a player switch; Jeff asks ahead (model input change)

- Jeff's default set-up changes to the one that played best in the 2026-11-10 trials: ask_ahead on, two-step intent and the undo note off, and step hints (`option_links`, links table v3) on. Each request then lists, for every option, the work it leads to (as the game computes it from the kitchen); options are unchanged. Two-step intent stays released, off; a server can still turn any of these on or off with `features.py`.
- Step hints have a player switch in Settings → AI teammate, on by default; a change applies from the next round, and a round played with the player's own setting records it as `"source": "player"` (research record and metadata schemas allow it).
- The links table counts a held pan scooping an ingredient off a counter as work (`option-links-v3`).
- The links table (option_links, option_links_partner) and the intent layer are merged from their trial branches; option_links_partner and the intent layer stay off.

### Two more decision services: Perplexity and Parallel

- Perplexity Decider (`pplx-decider-v1.2`, `https://api.perplexity.ai/v1/decisions`) is listed in the local game and online: its API accepts browser calls, with the key as Bearer and the model name in the request.
- Parallel Decisions (`https://api.parallel.ai/v1/decisions`, mode fast) is listed in the local game only: its API does not accept browser calls. The key goes in the `x-api-key` header and the request carries no model name (the API refuses other fields); a refusal to answer counts as a failed request. Services can now say which header carries the key (`key_header`) and that no model name is sent (`send_model: false`), in the local game and the online page alike.

### Controllers: one right Joy-Con, the controller named when it is not supported

- One right Joy-Con held sideways works in Safari ("Joy-Con (R) Gamepad", no standard mapping), with the same buttons as the left one: right takes and puts, left chops, washes or throws, bottom dashes, + pauses. The picture hint shows + for it.
- A controller the page cannot map is named in its notice (its id and mapping), so a player can say which one it is.
- The English controller docs said Y dashes and B cancels an aim; they now match the game (B dashes, no aim cancel).

### Drying racks, pans that scoop, level 4 rearranged (rule change, model input change)

- Every sink has a drying rack beside it, as Overcooked 2's washing station: dirty plates stack in the sink and stay there until washed (first in, washed first; none can be taken back out), each washed plate goes onto the rack (the newest on top), and Space at the sink with empty hands takes a clean plate from the rack. Both chefs play by it. Level 1 moves its stove and plate counter one cell right for the rack (its corner counter is now counter 2); levels 2 and 3 turn the counter beside the sink into it; on level 4 it stands on the sink's right. Maps, levels, the equipment catalog (`drying_rack`) and the ruleset move up a version; the contract and the level editor keep exactly one rack, beside the sink.
- A held empty pan or pot over a loose ingredient it can cook scoops it up and stays in the hands (`scoop <station>`), as a held plate takes an ingredient: one press for the player, one choice for Jeff.
- Level 4: the extinguisher moves to Jeff's side beside the stoves; the plate return moves right, five cells from the sink.
- Jeff's rules say how the sink and rack work and that a pan scoops (`rules-v14`).
- The level editor's sink tool places the sink with its rack as one unit; erasing either removes both.
- The sink unit turns to face the chef: its frames have the tap on the wall side for a chef below or to the left; a sink the chef reaches from above (level 4) or from the right is drawn mirrored, so the tap stays on the wall side. Only the steel mirrors; the plates on the rack and the wires holding them stay upright.
- The sink and rack are drawn as one steel unit across two cells from the art pack (`modular/sink_unit_<side>` in `grid-foundation-v1`): basin, tap and drain on the sink's cell, a drainboard with plates standing in the rack on the rack's cell, and a count when several dirty plates wait. A plate in the basin is drawn small enough to sit inside its rim, in line with the tap; a vertical unit shows its wash bar just outside its far end.
- Both chefs walk 4 cells per second (was 5), the pace chosen in playtesting.
- Walking into the other chef on purpose (`go partner`) ends only on a step that touches them, so it always pushes. At 4 cells a second the last step could stop a few thousandths of a cell short, and that used to count as arriving, with no push.
- Calibration on the new maps at the new walking pace: the scripted pair earns less, so by the calibration rule (half the median, rounded down to 10) level 2's target falls from 200 to 160, level 3's from 160 to 140 and level 4's from 100 to 90; level 1 stays at 200. Level 4 still needs both chefs (the solo script earns −80 to −40 on every seed). The benchmark moves to v3 (v2 retired: its levels play differently).

### Picking up as in Overcooked 2, a clearer target, plates drawn in flight

- A loose item is picked up within Overcooked 2's reach (InteractWithItemHelper: the half-disc ahead): within 1.2 cells of the chef and not behind, the one nearest a point half a cell ahead; the aimed tile still wins a tie, then the feet. Before, only the aimed tile and the feet counted, so running past an item beside the chef missed it. The page highlights the same item the engine takes (`tests/test_item_reach.py`). Stations are chosen as before (the four cells beside the chef's own, as Overcooked 2's grid selection).
- The targeted station with something to do is marked plainly: a bright fill, a thick blue frame and corner brackets just outside the tile. One with nothing to do keeps the quiet thin frame.
- A thrown or dropped plate is drawn as a plate. The engine's plate carries a default ingredient field, and the page looked up an ingredient picture that does not exist.

### Controllers: Switch Joy-Cons, dash on B, a picture hint

- A pair of Joy-Cons that macOS merges into one "Joy-Con (L/R)" device reports its left stick as a D-pad turned for a sideways Joy-Con (stick up came out as left); the page turns it back for the pair held upright. Safari reads the same pair through Apple's framework ("Joy-Con (L/R) Extended Gamepad") with the stick and arrows already upright, so it is left as it is.
- One left Joy-Con held sideways works in Safari ("Joy-Con (L) Gamepad", no standard mapping): the stick moves, the right button takes and puts, the left button chops, washes or throws, the bottom button dashes, − pauses or resumes. Other controllers without a standard mapping are still left alone.
- Dash moves to B (○), as in Overcooked 2; Y no longer dashes. B no longer cancels an aim: as in Overcooked 2, releasing X throws. The help, `docs/interaction-guide.md` and `docs/device-support.md` say so (the help still said X dashes).
- The controller hint is pictures instead of a sentence, as in Overcooked 2's prompts: each action is its button (letters, PlayStation shapes, or the lit button of a sideways Joy-Con) and a pixel picture (hand, cleaver, dust puffs, pause). It sits in a narrow column at the right edge and no longer covers the start and result cards; the words stay in its accessible label.

## 0.8.1-beta.1 — 2026-11-09 · fifth public test release

### Music and fixes

- The three music pieces are composed in code (`scripts/audio/compose_music.py`, `music_synth.py`): "午后小馆" before the round, the "厨房进行曲" march in service, and the same march in minor at 160 BPM for the last 30 seconds, with the in-round lead throughout. Service and rush start at full level, the rush on the service's next bar line; only a resume after a pause continues a piece in place.
- Music no longer stops after its first loop in some browsers: Chromium-based ones could freeze a looping buffer at its end, so a loop now ends a millisecond inside the buffer.
- Taking the extinguisher with full hands from across a counter row no longer breaks the round: the swapped-out item could land on the rack's own tile, which is not floor, and every later state failed. A swapped-out item now goes only to a floor tile.

### Level 4 behind a switch, off

- `level_switches.json` says which listed levels players see; it holds `{"level-4": {"on": false}}`, so level 4 leaves the page's level list and `/api/level` refuses it, while the engine, tests, research replay and the research console still have it. A level the file does not name is shown. A machine shows or hides a level without a new release through `/etc/chefjeff/levels.override.json` (or the path in `CHEFJEFF_LEVELS_OVERRIDE`), in the same shape and read again when it changes; a wrong override is not used. `python3 levels.py show` and `python3 levels.py set level-4 on|off` read and write it.

### Full-reach throws land on the far tile

- A throw that reached a floor tile only past its centre was cut short. A floor tile counted as reached only when its centre was within reach, while a station counted when its nearest edge was, so from a spot a little off a tile centre a full 4-tile throw fell a tile short. On level 4, with Jeff at a stove 0.15 off centre holding beef and the player 4.11 tiles away across the counter row, "throw partner" was not offered at all (every tile beside the player was a few thousandths of a tile too far for a missed catch to land on) and "throw down" came down on his own side. As in Overcooked 2, where a throw lands wherever it flies, a floor tile now counts as reached like a station; the same rule holds for both chefs. The behaviour and payload fingerprints are re-recorded: each recording first changes at a throw now landing a tile farther, the four reference rounds are unchanged, and every payload round keeps its result and request count.

### Food names and undo notes on by default (model input change)

- `food_names` (below) and `undo_note` (0.8.0-beta.1) are now on in `features.json`. Both stay trials, so a server can turn either off through its override file without a new release, and every round's record says which were on. They were checked together with the released one-press and two-step intent, and with the plan and reach_note trials, in model-free rounds: all four combinations ran to the end with all their notes and questions in each request. `undo_note` still keeps asking ahead from running; that trial is off. The payload fingerprints are re-recorded: only the food names, `rules.food_names`, `food_names_version` and the continuity rule's wording changed; every round's result, request count and request times are the same.

### Put down, then fetch (one-press v4, model input change)

- A new one-press option, `put then fetch <ingredient>` (the key is the fetch key with "put then " in front, e.g. `put then fetch beef`): while Jeff holds an item the engine lists no fetch, because fetching needs empty hands, so he had to choose a put and then, in a second request, the fetch. The option puts the held item where `put_where` says and fetches from that source, as two ordinary steps, like the put then take, chop and wash options. It is offered for each source whose fetch his full hands keep from the list, and not when the source's top is taken. A player makes the same two presses. Replayed offline at level 2's 146-second moment with a scripted partner, Jev holding a plate noted "fetch beef" as his next work while no option fetched. `one_press` is now `one-press-v4`; `put_where` now says it goes with any "put then ..." action. Engine, rules and the page are unchanged; the payload fingerprints change where Jeff holds an item.

### Food named by ingredient and state (trial)

- A trial, `food_names` in `features.json` (version `names-v1`, no player switch, off). Jeff's food names were the page's display names, translated; beef's dated from when it was the only ingredient, so raw beef reached him as "Raw meat", chopped beef as "Chopped" and cooked beef as "Cooked", while the recipes and a plate's missing list say "beef (cooked)", the fridge's fetch said "raw meat" and no station said what it supplies. On level 2 a scripted assembler partnered with Jev left him waiting in 57% of his answers, and fetching beef was not among his 20 most frequent answers; replayed offline, the request at such a moment names beef nowhere in a fetch or a station. With the switch on, every loose ingredient is named "<item> (<state>)" in the recipes' words, a plate by its dish when the round's orders can ask for it (else "plate with" its components), a pan or pot by its kind and contents, every action label that names food uses the same name, each ingredient source carries `supplies`, and `rules.food_names` says how food is named. Names are built from the ingredient ids and states, so all twelve ingredients a player level can use are named alike; `tests/test_food_names.py` lays out every state of each and fails on any food name or label that does not name its ingredient, or on Chinese anywhere in the request. With the switch off, every request is byte-identical; the page, the engine's events and the records' engine inputs are unchanged with it on. A new payload fingerprint, `level2-food-names`, plays the same round as `level2`; research replay rebuilds named requests exactly. `scripts/food_names_check.py` (`reask.py food_names`) replays logged rounds from their session bundles and re-asks the moments Jeff waited empty-handed while an ingredient the menu needs was nowhere, as logged and as the switch builds them.
- Player levels name their stations as the authored maps do (`level_design._names`: 柜台 3, 灶台 1, 灭火器架, 冰箱, 生菜箱; an ingredient source by its ingredient), where they used to show the station id. The same test found that a player level's extinguisher rack, named by its id, gave a take label the translation catalog did not know, so it reached Jeff half in Chinese ("去extinguisher拿Extinguisher"); now every label is translated, with the switch on or off. The five supply names missing from the catalogs (cucumber, onion, cheese, fish, flatbread) are added for Jeff and the page. A round already recorded keeps its frozen configuration and its station names, so its replay is unchanged.

### One key pattern for fetching

- Fetching from the first ingredient source, the fridge on every authored level, is now `fetch beef`, like `fetch lettuce`, `fetch tomato` and `fetch bread`; it used to be a bare `fetch`, from when beef was the only ingredient. With `food_names` on, the option read "Fetch beef (as fetched)" while its key stayed `fetch`, and a chat model answered `fetch beef`, which was refused as not offered. Every source is now fetched by what it supplies (`fetch <ingredient>`), and where two sources supply the same ingredient, a player's level for one, by both (`fetch beef fridge`, `fetch beef fridge2`), so the key alone says what is fetched; before, every other source was `fetch <station>`, which on a player's level could name a station whose ingredient Jeff had to look up. The engine plays exactly as before: decisions, events and results of every reference round are unchanged apart from the key, and a round recorded with an older key still replays (a recorded `fetch` or `fetch <station>` starts the same source's fetch). Jeff's requests list the new key, so every payload recording was re-recorded; the reference traces were too, since their checkpoints include a running job's key.
- Benchmark v2: v1 froze those reference traces, so it no longer matches the code and retires before anyone ran it. v2 measures what v1 did (levels 1–3, the scripted assembler, 12 rounds per level, env-v1); `benchmark/v1/manifest.json` is kept as it was, and `scripts/benchmark.py` defaults to v2.
- `scripts/food_names_check.py` rebuilds moments from rounds played on any model: it rebuilt every request with Jev's model name, so a round on DeepSeek or another connection never matched its logged request and was left out.

## 0.8.0-beta.1 — 2026-11-08 · fourth public test release

### Level 4: across the counter

- A new level, Level 4 · Across the counter (`content/levels/level-4.json`, map `maps/level-4.json`). A row of counters runs from wall to wall and splits the floor in two: the ingredients, two stoves, the serving window and a bin on one side, two boards, the sink, the dirty-plate return, the extinguisher rack and a second bin on the other, so every dish needs both chefs. Steak and burger, an order every 30 s, countdowns 60/90 s (the pacing rule of player levels). Calibrated on the run set `calibration-v2`: the scripted pair's median is ¥200 over order seeds 1–21 (¥108–230), so the target is ¥100; the solo script serves nothing on any seed (¥−80 to −40).
- Levels can say `requires_pair: true` (`schemas/level.schema.json`); Level 4 does. `tests/test_level_four.py` reads the committed calibration report and fails if, for such a level, any solo run reaches the target, the pair's median does not, or the entry was made for another run set or another configuration of the level. Each level's entry in `docs/architecture/reports/calibration.json` now records the configuration hash it was made for and whether the level requires a pair. Levels 1–3 do not claim it: one scripted chef reaches the target on Level 1 on every seed, on Level 2 on 10 of 21 and on Level 3 on 19 of 21; the README now says so, and that cooperation is tested on Level 4.
- `scripts/decision_stats.py` (report `decision-stats-17`) adds a cooperation gain to the outcomes and to `--by-round`: a round's net revenue minus the solo script's calibration median for the round's exact configuration hash, labelled "relative to the solo script baseline"; rounds on a configuration the report has no entry for are counted in `rounds_without_baseline`.
- Hosted usage statistics count Level 4 on its own; a region on an older release may still report levels 1–3 only. New behaviour fingerprints `level4-zoned` and `level4-greedy`; the baseline ladder in `docs/architecture/collaboration-analyzer.md` is rerun on all four levels; the help tab lists Level 4 and the current targets.
- Level 4 rounds can now be recorded for research: the record and package schemas allowed levels 0-3 only, so every level-4 record failed validation and the result screen said the contribution failed. The range now ends at 4, and a test keeps it covering every level in `content/levels`. Upgrade the research console with this release.
- New trial `reach_note` (`features.json`, version `reach-v1`, no player switch, off by default). The player sees the counters split the floor; Jeff gets walls and equipment as coordinates. With it on, a request on a split floor gets one rule of fact, `rules.reach`, listing the stations only Jeff can reach, only the human can reach and both can reach, as the engine decides it, with nothing about passing food or dividing work. On a floor in one piece (levels 1-3) requests are unchanged byte for byte. The rule is the same for the whole round, so the compact format sends it in the static part; research replay reads the switch from `conditions.features`.
- A new re-ask check, `python3 scripts/reask.py reach_note logs/` (`scripts/reach_note_check.py`): from local round journals it picks the moments on a split floor at which Jeff held a raw ingredient he could not prepare on his side, so the only way on was to pass it, and asks each once as logged and once with `reach_note` (next_action only in both), reporting how often the answer passed, the probability the model put on the passing options and its change per moment. It can ask a decision service on this computer (`--local`, no key), StartLux on Bamboo (`--bamboo`, key read from `~/.config/chefjeff/bamboo.key`, which only its owner may read, never printed) or StartLux's demo Space on Hugging Face (`--hf-space`, no key). The report holds counts, shares and probabilities only.

### Asking Jeff ahead (off by default)
- A trial, `ask_ahead` in `features.json` (version `ask-ahead-v1`, no player switch, off). Today, when a reply could only come after Jeff's step ends, the loop waits for the step to end and Jeff then stands for the whole reply time. With the trial on, the loop asks at that moment about the kitchen as it will be at the step's end: a fork of the kitchen (`Kitchen.fork`, with no record hooks and route caches, inputs and random state of its own) is played forward to the end of the step. The answer is carried out when the step ends if the step ended as predicted, Jeff's hand and the countdown bands at its end match the prediction, the action is still listed and the answer is not too old; otherwise it is dropped and Jeff is asked at once as before. Predicting before every tick of every fingerprint round leaves every behaviour and payload fingerprint byte-identical. In an offline level-2 simulation (a stand-in model replying in exactly 1.2 or 3.5 s), the median wait between Jeff's steps went from 1.25 to 0.78 s and from 3.55 to 2.55 s. Requests per round rose from 89 to 106 and from 40 to 50, no answer was refused by the engine, and of the answers asked ahead 48 of 48 and 25 of 30 were carried out (5 dropped as no longer listed). Most steps now last about a second, shorter than a reply, so one step of look-ahead saves at most the step's own length. Requests record `ask_ahead`, answers `ask_ahead_result`, research replay redoes the prediction (100% rebuilt in the tests), `request_policy.ask_ahead` marks the rounds, and `scripts/decision_stats.py` (report `decision-stats-16`) counts their step gaps as an `ask_ahead` group with a negative `waiting_to_ask`. A new payload fingerprint, `level2-ask-ahead`, has replies taking 1.2 s.

### Hosted model requests by long poll
- The hosted page now asks for Jeff's model requests by long poll (`GET /api/model/request?wait=1`): the server holds the answer up to 12 seconds and answers as soon as a request is set, so the page no longer asks five times a second and waits on average 100 ms more per decision. A waiting poll holds neither the kitchen's lock nor a shared request slot (one per page, no more than the kitchens running), stays out of the latency percentiles (`/ops/capacity` counts `model_long_polls`) and closes its connection after; a quick empty answer (an older server) waits the old 200 ms, an error 1 s. Requests without `wait` answer at once as before. `scripts/load_test_hosted.py` runs each page's model requests in a loop of its own as the page does, with `--long-poll` for the new way, and reports `/api/state` latency and 503s while pages play apart. Hosted records note `request_policy.relay_long_poll`.

### Jeff's input (model input change, off by default)
- A compact request format on trial: `compact_input` in `features.json` (version `compact-v1`, no player switch, off). With it on, every client (local chat and decision clients, the hosted Jev relay, the free models and Cloudflare, and the hosted page, which gets both parts from the server) sends the request as `static`, what stays the same for the whole round, placed first (the system message for chat models, `state.static` for decision services), and `live`, the rest. In the payload fingerprints `static` is one byte-identical text per round for every client, including rounds with player messages, plans and one-press, and `live` is 20–25% of today's message. The request itself is unchanged: journals, research records, replays and analysis see the same payload, `wire_format.expand` gives back its state exactly on every fingerprint request, and with the switch off every client sends today's bytes. A seventh payload fingerprint, `level2-compact`, has the same payload sections as `level2` and different request bodies for every client. Local chat clients record `cached_input_tokens` when the provider reports prompt cache hits.

### Jeff
- A server can switch Jeff's features without a new release: `python3 features.py set NAME on|off` (and `unset`, `show`, `check`) writes `/etc/chefjeff/features.override.json`, which may set only `on` and `player_switch` of the release's features and is refused when it breaks the file's rules. No restart: new rounds take it, rounds already running keep theirs. A feature set this way is recorded with `"source": "server"` (the record and package schemas allow it).
- The two-step intent (`rolling_intent`, rolling-v3) is released and on in every round: with each next step Jeff also says which piece of work it serves and which he plans next, and that intent goes into his next request; it is hidden from him when a pan is about to burn. The two Settings switches, 计划模式 (plan mode) and 两步打算 (two-step intent), are gone, and `jeff_plan` stays off. The evidence is two small level-2 comparisons (2026-11-04, 3 rounds each: walk-backs 5.0 → 3.15 per game minute; 2026-11-08, one round each: less wasted work, more revenue). It does not stop the level-4 swaps where Jeff keeps raw meat on a counter only he can reach. Research records still carry each round's plan mode in `conditions.features` and `jeff_plan`, so rounds before and after tell apart.
- The route points in Jeff's input are now rounded to four decimals, like positions. They used to be full floats, whose last digit can differ between machines: 1 of the 1189 server requests could not be rebuilt exactly on another machine for that reason. Rules and Jeff's actions are unchanged.
- New trial switch `undo_note` in `features.json`, off by default and with no switch in the page. With it on, an option that would take back what Jeff's last step put down, or put back what it took, says so in its label with the time, as a fact; nothing is forbidden and the player sees no note. ## 0.6.0-beta.1 — 2026-10-01 · first public test release

The game now runs on a data-driven core: five data models (map, recipes, orders, equipment, level) describe every level, and the engine has no per-level code.

### Rules
- One rule set for every level: each round lasts 180 game seconds and the goal is net revenue at closing. Orders keep arriving until closing, and orders still open at closing carry no penalty.
- Burnt dishes: burnt for up to 5 s is accepted at the price −10; burnt longer is refused and the order keeps waiting. Serving a dish no order is waiting for costs 20.
- The 0.5.9 rules are removed: bad reviews, the delivery-count goal, ending the round as soon as the goal is met, and the remaining-time bonus.
- Level 1 is the practice level; Levels 2 and 3 are calibrated against a reference pair.

### Kitchen and controls
- Overcooked-style keyboard controls with browser-safe keys: Space acts on what the chef faces (hold it to aim a throw), Shift dashes, Enter bookmarks.
- Beef fries in a frying pan; the engine also supports a soup pot for noodles, and each stove holds one vessel.
- Chefs can pass plates, dishes, pans and the extinguisher up to 4 tiles; ingredients are thrown up to 4 tiles.
- Assemble with a plate or ingredient lying on the floor, as on a counter.
- One chef body size against every workstation: chefs stay off cabinet fronts and work from where walking stops.
- Walking slides around shallow notches; stalled routes re-plan around the other chef; "go partner" walks into the other chef on purpose.

### Jeff (the AI teammate)
- Clear chef names (`human`, `jeff`) and fewer duplicate choices in Jeff's input; a plate's missing parts name the state they need.
- Jeff's rules are generated from the same frozen configuration as the engine, with no collaboration-value wording.

### Records and analysis
- Every round is recorded as one event stream and a replayable session bundle.
- End-of-round record of who did which work, shown after closing.
- Item provenance and collaboration analysis. The round record shows three experimental measures: contribution by standard time (configured work plus the shortest walk needed), delay beyond the ideal split between the chefs, and idle time. The analyzer also reports each dish's critical path.
- Capacity Analyzer and a calibration ladder (solo, solo with random partner, pair).
- Behaviour fingerprints of the three levels guard against unintended rule changes.

### Presentation
- Kitchen UI design system, pixel font, music and sound effects.
- Both chefs are drawn from one master body per view, so they keep their size while chopping; the knife is its own layer.
- Eight more ingredients (cucumber, onion, cheese, scallion, chicken, fish, flatbread, noodles) and six recipes with pixel art, ready for new levels.
- Burnt dishes are recognisable wherever they are.

### 在线版模型请求改为长轮询
- 在线页面改用长轮询取 Jeff 的模型请求（`GET /api/model/request?wait=1`）：服务器最多挂起 12 秒，一有请求就立刻返回，页面不再每秒问五次，每次决策也少等平均 100 毫秒。挂起的请求不占这一局的锁，也不占共享请求名额（每个页面一个，总数不超过正在运行的厨房数），不计入延迟分位数（`/ops/capacity` 记为 `model_long_polls`），返回后关闭连接；很快返回空结果（旧服务器）时等原来的 200 毫秒，出错时等 1 秒。不带 `wait` 的请求照旧立刻返回。`scripts/load_test_hosted.py` 像页面一样让每个页面在单独的循环里取模型请求，加 `--long-poll` 用新方式，并单独报告 `/api/state` 的延迟和页面游玩期间的 503。在线对局的记录会写明 `request_policy.relay_long_poll`。

### 研究记录
- `scripts/replay_release.py check <研究包>` 用录下这一局的那个版本的代码回放研究记录。它先按记录里的版本指纹在 `deploy/release-fingerprints.json` 里查对应的提交，查不到就逐个计算更早提交的版本指纹，找到后记进这个表。然后用 `git archive` 把那次提交的代码取到私有缓存里，在那里回放。报告只有局号和计数。`replay_release.py record <提交>` 把一个已部署的版本记进表里。服务器上目前所有带回放数据的 28 局（版本号都标为 0.6.0-beta.2，实际是 5 个不同的构建）用各自的提交都能完全回放，1189 次请求里有 1188 次能原样重建。
- 对局被中途结束时（关掉页面、长时间没操作、手动结束或重开），"中途结束"标记是会话直接改的，不经过引擎输入，所以这类局最后一个状态以前过不了回放检查。现在 `research_replay.check` 从记录里读这个标记。

### 页面
- 本地游戏的设置里多了一项服务「本机决策模型」（`model-providers.json` 里的 `local-decision`，线上网站没有）：连这台电脑上的 systemone 决策服务，比如 StartLux 2B，填完整地址（`http://127.0.0.1:8090/v1/systemone`）。只接受 localhost、`127.0.0.1` 和 `[::1]`，可以用 HTTP；不填 Key 就不发 Key，也绝不发 Jev 的 Key。因为用 CPU 算一次可能要几分钟，一次决策最多可以等 600 秒（`timeout_seconds`），不再按 `ai_timeout_seconds`。超过 `ai_max_response_age` 的回复仍然照旧作废，所以实时对局里这么慢的模型给出的回答会被丢掉。研究记录接受这个服务名。
- 本地游戏（不是线上网站）可以连本机上的模型：自定义模型的地址填 `http://localhost`、`127.0.0.1` 或 `[::1]` 加端口时，允许用 HTTP，Key 可以不填，不填就不发 Key（也不会改用 `.env` 里的 Jev Key）。其他内网地址仍然拒绝，公网地址仍然必须是 HTTPS 并填 Key。
- 放在面包箱上的生肉下面不再出现切菜进度条。页面以前看工位名字是不是以 b 开头来判断案板，面包箱（`bread`）也被当成了案板；现在按工位类型判断，放东西的声音也一样。
- 页面现在检查厨房服务的 `client_protocol`（`release_info.CLIENT_PROTOCOL`，放在 `release` 里下发），不再比较写死在 `KitchenClient.ts` 里的版本号。页面更新后仍在运行的旧服务，或约定不同的服务，照样显示「等待厨房更新」；但只要页面与服务器之间的约定没变，发新版本就不必重新构建网页。前端包用 `scripts/rebuild_presentation.cjs` 重新编译，在下一次用 Creator 构建之前，主脚本没有压缩（gzip 后大约多 25 KB）。

### 研究工具
- 每个重问检查报告比例时，都会说明这个比例有多可靠。`research_stats.py`（只用标准库）提供 Wilson 区间、带固定种子的 bootstrap 区间、成对是/否结果的精确 McNemar 检验，以及"看出某个差别每组需要多少个时刻或多少局"的估算，都对照已发表的数值做了测试。`scripts/reask.py` 放各检查共用的部分。一是同一个提问循环：每组对每个时刻问同样多次，顺序固定，并记录作答的模型。二是每份报告里的 `stats` 部分，包括：每个指标的比例和 95% 区间；与第一组的成对差别和 McNemar p 值；模型两次给出相同回答的比例；全部回答来自同一个模型时给出提示。`walk_label_check`、`hands_full_check`、`put_where_check`、`undo_note_check` 和 `jev_replay_check` 都通过它提问，`plan_probe` 也加上了区间。用固定输入检验过，原有报告字段全部不变。`python3 scripts/reask.py list` 列出所有检查，`reask.py <检查名> ...` 运行其中一个。
- `scripts/model_rounds.py` 让脚本扮演的人类（`reference_policy`：assembler、cook、solo 或 idle）和真实的 Jeff 打完整对局。对局使用本地游戏自己的会话，按真实时间进行，用游戏里保存的连接。这就是基线阶梯的第 4 级，预算允许跑多少局就跑多少局。日志和会话包写到 `--out`（不会写进 `logs/`），能精确回放，`decision_stats.py` 也能读取；`--dry-run` 只打印这组对局最多花多少钱，不调用任何模型。
- `docs/research/trial-protocol.md` 说明测量一个试验开关前要先写下什么：一个主要指标、底线指标、样本量、至少两个模型、预算和决策规则，并附两个填好的例子，分别是紧凑输入和提前问。举例来说，要分辨 0.75 和 0.60 的重复一致率，每组大约需要 152 个时刻。
- 读日志的测试改为自己设置 `CHEFJEFF_LOG_DIR`，不再修改 `play.ROOT`；开发者在自己的终端里设置了这个变量时，测试也能通过。
- `scripts/decision_stats.py`（报告版本 `decision-stats-18`）新增 `uncertainty` 部分，给出这些数的 95% 区间：达标率、选了最高概率选项的比例、被 Jeff 自己下一个选择替换掉的步骤比例（Wilson 区间）；每局平均收入、协作增益、Jeff 的标准时间占比、各请求策略下步间空等的中位数（带固定种子的 bootstrap）。其余字段全部不变，加不加 `--by-round`、`--split` 都一样。

### 环境接口与 Benchmark v1
- agent 现在通过环境接口（`environment.py`，`env-v1`）来玩，不再直接拿厨房对象。`AgentPort` 分两层。第一层是任何 agent 都可以依赖的普通数据：`observe()` 给出英文的厨房状态（去掉只供页面显示的字段）、不裁剪的全部合法动作和事件序号；`act()` 在动作到达时校验，可以附带"只有还在做那件事时才执行"的保护。第二层是给本进程 agent 用的只读引擎视图，所有引擎输入都会被拒绝，所以 agent 做的任何事都是有记录的输入，能精确回放。Jeff 的决策循环改为通过只读视图读、通过端口执行；发给他的内容和引擎的每个决定都逐字节不变（payload 指纹、行为指纹、研究回放都核对过）。`agent_loop.py` 能在游戏会话里运行任何 `environment.Agent`；`example_agent.py` 是一个只用 `observe()` 和 `act()` 的完整 agent，是用来超越的底线，不是好厨师。详见 `docs/architecture/environment-api.md`。
- Benchmark v1（`benchmark/v1/manifest.json`，`scripts/benchmark.py run|verify|report`）：第 1–3 关、脚本搭档 `assembler`、每关 12 局、真实时间。可以让 Jeff 用你的模型来跑（`--agent jeff`），也可以跑你自己的 agent（`--agent 模块:类名`）。每份结果都保存每一局的会话包和回放检查结果，并报告带 95% 区间的收入和达标率、相对脚本单人的增益，以及 agent 承担的工作占比。清单冻结了接口版本、每关的配置哈希、黄金参考轨迹的哈希和搭档策略。代码与清单不一致时测试会失败；以下几种结果 `verify` 会拒收：为别的清单生成的、局数不对的、用虚拟时钟跑的、有局不能回放的。`--dry-run` 估算一次运行的费用（36 局，108 分钟）。第四关在 v1.1 加入。详见 `benchmark/README.md`。
- `release_info.RUNTIME_FILES` 现在包含 `environment.py` 和 `agent_loop.py`，并新增测试：运行时文件引用的每个项目模块都必须在这个列表里。

- Jeff 的输入集中到一个模块 `harness.py` 里拼装：规则说明（每段只写一次，网格厨房的写法在原位置按条件选择）、列出的可选动作，以及每次请求追加的内容；各客户端只负责发送。输入逐字节不变：输入指纹和对非网格厨房的单独比对都一致。
- Jeff 的计划：设置 → AI 搭档里的测试开关，默认关闭：在 `scripts/plan_probe.py` 里用决策模型测，手里有一份不相关的计划时，锅快糊了它照着计划去取生菜（5 次都是），计划关闭时 5 次都去拿盘子；已经没有订单需要的计划，它也不放弃。Jeff 没有计划时，那次请求会多问接下来打算按顺序做的几道工序：最多 4 步，从本关的工序里选（取、切、下锅、装盘某种食材，拿干净盘子，出餐，收脏盘，洗盘子），第 2 步起可以选“没有了”。Jeff 每做完一个对应工序的动作，那一步就打勾，不要求顺序。计划没有时限，也不分工：全部做完、Jeff 在有计划时每次附带的“继续还是放弃”小问题里选了放弃，或者玩家发出新的偏好消息（1–5）时结束，随后像一步做完时那样马上问新的计划。计划不增减 Jeff 的任何动作，做计划外的事也不影响计划。电脑上的沟通区和手机顶部工具栏显示这串步骤和勾。没有回答或回答不在选项里，就当没有计划（或保留原计划），动作照常执行。之前有一版没有上线的做法，让 Jeff 认领一段分工 12 秒；本地对局里，那一段没事可做时它就一直等；装盘不归它管时，锅里的牛肉熟了它也不去拿，快糊了还在等玩家来拿。
- 计划开关关闭时，Jeff 的输入逐字节不变。打开时请求带 `plan_rules_version` `plan-v2`，第五份输入指纹覆盖第二关计划打开的情况。本地版、在线版和浏览器端读取计划题（`plan_step_1` 到 `plan_step_4`、`plan_keep`）的方式一致，智谱的结构化输出也包括在内；浏览器把回答放在结果的 `plan` 里发回。`scripts/decision_stats.py` 统计计划定了几次、怎么结束、每份几步、持续多久，以及有计划和没计划时 Jeff 选等待的比例。`scripts/plan_probe.py` 在你自己的电脑上用真实模型检查，计划会不会让 Jeff 不去处理两种固定的突发情况（锅快糊了，端锅、拿干净盘子和装盘都算处理；灶台着火），以及计划已经没用时（第一关订单全部出完以后）Jeff 会不会放弃它。
- 新增 `scripts/decision_stats.py`，按模型统计本机对局日志里 Jeff 怎么做决定：第一、第二选项概率差得很近的次数，动作还没做完就被它自己的下一个选择换掉的次数，在两个目标之间来回改的次数；打开计划时，还统计它的动作和计划那一段是否一致，以及第三档设想的触发条件（计划已经定了超过 5 秒时又拿不准）会触发多少次。它能读本机日志（`logs/`）、用 `scripts/pull_research.py` 拉下来的研究数据包（先核对，过期的会拒绝）和单局下载的研究记录；研究数据里的模型名是浏览器报告的，计划是怎么结束的没有记录。只记录：它不改任何东西，也不调用模型。

### 厨房与操作
- 按《胡闹厨房 2》的两个主键分工：空格（原作的拿起键，手柄 A）拿、放、装盘、出餐，按下立即操作，手里拿着东西也一样；E（原作的切/扔键，原作是左 Ctrl，网页里 Ctrl 加 W 会关掉标签页；手柄 X）切菜、洗碗、灭火，手持可投掷物品时点按朝前扔、按住瞄准。空格不再切菜、洗碗或扔东西，手柄冲刺改到 Y。手机也是两个大键，「拿/放」（原来的「操作」）和「切/洗/扔」（原来的「扔」），再加「冲刺」。以前空格能切就先切，所以玩家拿不走菜板上的生食材，Jeff 却能拿。
- 玩家拿放东西后不再背对工位。按空格或 E 时厨师会朝工位探一下身，收回时是往离开工位的方向挪几个像素，网页把这一下当成了走路，把厨师转了过去，直到下一次按键才转回来。现在照《胡闹厨房 2》，玩家自己的厨师只按方向键转身：按键探身、放东西或切菜前挪到工位正前方、被冲刺撞开、被 Jeff 推开和位置校正都不会让他转身。画出来的朝向统一由 `KitchenGeometry.ts` 里的一个函数决定，测试会逐帧重放四个方向的探身。
- 操作更跟手，照《胡闹厨房 2》改。拿放后不再停顿 0.35 秒：厨师马上能走，拿放由引擎照常完成（原作只在切菜、洗碗等工位操作时和瞄准投掷时不能走）。拿起东西后的 0.5 秒只限制再拿一样，和原作的拿起间隔一样；刚拿起的东西可以马上放下（以前这 0.5 秒里按什么都没反应）。拿放完成的那一刻网页会立刻再取一次状态，不再等下一次 0.2 秒的轮询，东西到手最多快 0.2 秒。
- 两位厨师都按每真实秒 5 格走路。这是用两段《胡闹厨房 2》实况录像、以柜台为尺量出来的原作走路速度（每秒约 5 到 5.3 个柜台；原作脚本里的走速和格子大小写在预制件数据里，读不到）。走路按真实时间计算：游戏时钟比现实慢时（0.5 和 0.75 两档），每游戏秒走的格数相应增加。以前本地每真实秒只走 3.4 格。对局把时钟倍速记成一条引擎输入（`set_clock`，研究记录格式的输入种类已加上），回放时走得和原局完全一样；旧记录照旧回放。请求录像和标准对局录像已重录，参考组合的收入仍是 300、318、390。两条会以中文出现在 Jeff 输入里的引擎消息（不在工位正前方、共同操作位置被占）补上了英文。
- 本地和线上一样按现实时钟运行，和《胡闹厨房 2》一致（以前本地是 0.75 倍，所有计时在本地都慢三分之一）。厨房里的时长照《胡闹厨房 2》脚本的默认值改：切菜 2.2 秒（原来 3 秒；切 8 刀，每秒约 3.7 刀），煎牛肉 10 秒（原来 12 秒），熟后 10 秒烧糊，糊的同时灶台起火（原来再过 8 秒），洗一个盘子 2 秒（原来 4 秒），顾客的盘子 10 秒后送回（原来 8 秒），拿和放瞬间完成（原来 0.15 秒），两位厨师相同。火的蔓延和复燃本来就和原作一致。订单等待时间改为关卡编辑器的节奏公式按新时长算出的值：第一、三关牛排 60 秒（原来 75 秒），第三关汉堡 90 秒（原来 105 秒）；第二关仍是 120 秒。各关目标按新时长重新校准（`calibration.json`）。
- 订单照《胡闹厨房 2》（`ServerOrderControllerBase`）来：开局就有两张，之后每隔本关的间隔（30、40、30 秒）来一张，等待中的少于两张时立刻补一张，所以出餐快，下一张就来得快；最多同时 5 张。订单超时仍扣 10 元，但不消失，重新计时（`ResetOrderLifetime`）；以前订单按固定时间来（每局 5 或 6 张），超时就消失。订单规则新增 `keep_open`、`max_open` 和 `on_expire`，不写时仍按计划的固定时间。Jeff 的规则说明加了订单怎么来（`rules-v11`）。订单不再限量，目标改为按 Jeff 每 3 秒决定一次的参考组合校准，更接近真人和模型搭档的速度（`calibration-v2`）：第一关 ¥200（原 150），第二关 ¥200（原 160），第三关 ¥160（原 190）。
- 灭一处火只需喷 0.5 秒，和《胡闹厨房 2》的默认值一样（原来 4 秒）；蔓延和复燃不变。
- 空格和 E 选工位的方式照《胡闹厨房 2》（`GetFacingGridOccupant`）：只看角色所在格上下左右四格，斜对角不算，最符合瞄准方向的优先；前面是空地时，侧面的工位（最多偏 135°）也会被选中，所以站在案板上方顺着柜台朝向别处时，按 E 会切这块案板，和原作一样；身后那格永远不选。以前看周围 8 格、侧面永远不选，网页还可能用上排第二的工位。朝左右瞄准时仍按手所在的那一格算，和画出来的刀一致。Jeff 的走位不变。
- 把东西放在脚下时，厨师原地不动、朝向不变，和《胡闹厨房 2》一样（只是松手）；以前会被挪回格子中间，所以 Jeff 为了切菜先放下手里的东西后，会隔着半格切。站在后半格（最多半格）开始切菜或洗碗时，现在会先往前滑到贴边，手在工位正上方；能操作的范围不变。
- 菜板上的食材还没开始切或已经切好时可以拿走，切到一半谁都拿不走（《胡闹厨房 2》的 `ServerWorkstation.CanPickupItem`）。Jeff 的规则说明也写上了这一条（`rules-v10`）。
- 和《胡闹厨房 2》一样，案板就是能切菜的柜台，食材箱顶上也能放一件东西。柜台上能放的东西，包括装好菜的盘子和锅，都能放在案板或食材箱上，柜台上的操作在那里也都能做；只有需要切的生食材才会被切。食材箱上放着东西时，按空格拿起的是那件东西，要等顶上空了才能再取料；手里拿着东西对着空食材箱按空格，会把东西放上去。扔出去的食材也能落在空食材箱上。Jeff 有同样的动作（在案板和食材箱上放下、拿起、装盘、加料、合盘、入锅），规则升到 `rules-v9`，加了几句说明。
- 切菜和洗碗时手要在工位正上方：脚离某一侧的中线不超过半格（按侧移到中线之后的位置算），最多比平时的站位往后半格。从台面上隔壁一格或斜对角对着案板，不会再对着空气切菜，按空格会提示“站到案板正前方才能切”。和《胡闹厨房 2》一样，两位厨师可以一起切同一块案板、一起洗同一个水槽，单面案板也能并排，进度相加；Jeff 也能这样加入，对方站在正前方时它会站到旁边。Jeff 的规则升到 `rules-v8`，多了一句说明这件事。
- 松开方向键时，自己的厨师保持最后按的方向，和《胡闹厨房》一样。以前会先转回服务器那边还没更新的旧朝向，再转回来，线上短距离挪位置时最明显。
- 灭火改成和《胡闹厨房 2》一样，两位厨师规则相同：喷会降低火势，走开后进度保留在工位上，几次短喷可以累计到 4 秒；停喷 2 秒后火会在 5 秒内长回满。工位上的红色条显示剩下多少。开始喷时摇杆还按着的方向不会打断灭火。规则集新增 `fire.recovery_game_ms`。
- 按住「扔」时按钮不会被禁用，手机上总能松手结束瞄准；服务器拒绝的扔会留下记录。
- 第二个人可以从任意另一侧空位加入一起切菜、洗碗，包括站在第一个人对面，不再只限于和他成直角的一侧：第一关中间的 2 号案板现在也能两人一起切。两位厨师规则相同。
- README 新增「在线试玩」（https://chefjeff.online/），触屏操作单独列表，和键盘操作并列。
- 在线试玩改到 https://chefjeff.online/（带 www 也可以），原来的地址 chefjeff.belchingleo.online 会跳转过去。更新脚本会一次性把服务改到新地址，更新失败则改回去；两个地址的证书续期后都会重新加载 Nginx。
- 和《胡闹厨房 2》一样，火只让柜台、案板不能“使用”，不影响台面：上面的东西照样能拿走（放在柜台上的灭火器遇火后不会再被困住），也能往上放、装盘，扔过去也能落在上面。着火的灶台也一样：锅可以端走、放回去，空锅还能加料，但灭火前不加热，灭火后没糊的食物接着加热。在着火的案板上切菜、从着火的取料处取料，仍要先灭火。着火工位上的东西会画在火焰下面，不再消失。
- 修复：扔的落点如果是一块中心略超出射程的案板或柜台，这个扔法会被列出来，按下去却被拒绝，提示“抛递目标已变化”。前三关列出的扔法里约有 2% 到 3% 属于这种情况，玩家和 Jeff 都会遇到。现在列出和执行用同一个射程判断，扔到工位方格以内就落在上面。
- 修复：端出一道没有订单在等的菜（扣 20 元，两位厨师都一样）时，那个出餐口上方会弹出红字「送错 -¥20」，并播放出餐失败的音效。以前只有收入数字会变，因为这个音效登记在一个厨房早已不再发出的旧事件名下。糊菜被顾客拒收（不付钱，订单继续等）时，那个出餐口上方也会弹出红字「拒收」，音效相同；以前画面上什么都看不出来。

### 关卡校准
- 校准、基线阶梯和地图检查用的脚本厨师，改为从关卡数据里读每道菜要什么、每口锅能把什么从哪一步煮熟，不再只会做牛排和汉堡。所以菜谱目录里的任何一道菜都能校准，包括同时要用汤锅和平底锅的菜，以及完全不用下锅的菜。前三关的每一步决定都没有变，21 个种子的校准结果和行为指纹都和原来一致。
- 引擎判断火会不会蔓延时，原来每一帧都要重新找一遍每个工位挨着谁。因为一局里地图不会变，现在只找一次。结果完全相同，一局快了约五倍：第三关校准在 4 个进程下从 268 秒降到 54 秒。
- 新增 `level_calibration.py`，按发布版的规则给玩家做的关卡算订单节奏和目标：每 30 秒来一单，顾客耐心是这道菜切和煮总时间的 5 倍（60–135 秒，第一关和第三关算出来和原来完全一样），目标是脚本双人在 7 个订单种子上收入中位数的一半。价格不在 10–150 之间、地图做不出来的菜，会在开跑前被拦下；脚本双人一道菜都做不出的关卡不能玩，一个人也能过的关卡会加提示。脚本厨师挪到仓库根目录，跟着服务器一起发布。
- 玩家关卡按内容生成编号（关卡、地图、菜单和菜都用 `player-` 加 12 位十六进制），不会和官方关卡或别的设计撞号。在线统计和研究记录把所有玩家关卡记作第 0 关，具体是哪一张，研究记录里存的完整配置写得清楚。还在旧版本上的地区服务器可以继续只报第 1–3 关；研究后台把第 0 关显示为“玩家关卡”。
- 玩家关卡可以提交校准了：`POST /api/player-level/submit` 当场检查设计并排进队列，`GET /api/player-level/status` 返回排队位置，算完后返回订单节奏和目标。一次只校准一张，在单独的低优先级进程里跑；每个页面同时只能排一张，最多 8 张等候，超过 300 秒自动停止；算过的设计直接用之前的结果。本地服务器默认提供；线上服务器要加 `--player-levels` 才打开。说明见 docs/architecture/player-levels.md。
- 校准好的玩家关卡可以开局了：`POST /api/player-level/play` 在两局之间把当前厨房换成这张关卡，用法和换关一样；重开一局仍是这张关卡，用 `/api/level` 换回官方关卡。Jeff 读不到玩家写的任何文字：玩家的菜改成 `dish_1`、`dish_2`……名字是英文配料组合（“bread + chopped lettuce + cooked chicken”），玩家自己起的菜名单独存放，只给页面显示。为了放下这些配料名，菜名长度上限放宽到 160 个字符。校准相关的提示都加了英文翻译。
- 关卡编辑器的设计稿在服务器上变成完整关卡（`level_design.py`）：房间尺寸、墙、设备和菜，生成地图、开局物品（灭火器、3 个盘子、每个灶台配好的平底锅或汤锅）和菜谱。网页只需要交一份简单的设计稿，所有检查都在服务器这一处。以下情况会被拦下：墙把房间隔断；水槽、脏盘回收、灭火器架不是正好各 1 个；出餐口超过 2 个；柜台少于 3 个；设备跟前没有地方站。`build_map` 支持在房间里面画墙。
- 柜台现在可以把厨房完全隔成两块，墙仍然不行。地图检查改为：除墙以外的格子必须连通，可走地面可以是一块或两块。地面分成两块时，两位厨师各出生在一块，每人只会列出自己走得到的动作，路线也只走自己那一块；配置检查要求两块地面之间扔得过去。分区配合的脚本厨师会在自己这边出餐、把洗好的盘子送过去、必要时把菜扔到对面地上，所以这类厨房也能校准。有两个出餐口时，出餐提示指向走得到的最近一个。地板连成一片的厨房（包括前三关）每一步决定都不变。

## 0.6.0-beta.1 — 2026-10-01 · 首次公开测试版

游戏改为运行在数据驱动内核上：每一关都由五个数据模型（地图、菜谱、订单、设备、关卡）描述，引擎里没有针对某一关的代码。

### 规则
- 所有关卡使用同一套规则：每局 180 游戏秒，目标是关店时的净收入。订单持续到关店，关店时仍未完成的订单不扣钱。
- 糊菜：糊了 5 秒以内的按价格减 10 元收下；糊得更久会被拒收，订单继续等待。送出没有订单在等的菜扣 20 元。
- 删除 0.5.9 的旧规则：差评、出餐数目标、达标立即结束、剩余时间奖励。
- 第一关是练习关；第二、三关按参考双人组校准。

### 厨房与操作
- 胡闹厨房式键盘操作，按键避开浏览器冲突：空格对面前的东西操作（按住可瞄准投掷），Shift 冲刺，Enter 标记。
- 牛肉用平底锅煎；引擎也支持煮面条的汤锅，一个灶台同时只放一口锅。
- 盘子、菜、平底锅和灭火器可在 4 格内传递；食材可在 4 格内抛出。
- 盘子或食材放在地上时，也能像在柜台上一样组装。
- 厨师对所有工位使用同一身体尺寸：不再踩进柜台正面，走到哪里停下就在哪里操作。
- 行走时能滑过浅凹口；卡住的路线会绕开对方重新规划；“走向搭档”可以主动走到对方身边。

### Jeff（AI 队友）

- Jeff 看到的规则和引擎来自同一份冻结配置，不含任何“应该合作”的价值措辞。

### 记录与分析
- 每局记录为一条事件流，并生成可回放的对局包。

- 容量分析器，以及校准阶梯（单人、单人加随机搭档、双人）。
- 三关的行为指纹，防止规则被无意改动。
