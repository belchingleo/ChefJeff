## Unreleased

### Start, results and settings
- The start and result screens are one shop card. A level plate shows the dish, level and goal; ◀ ▶ change level and the title row (第 N 关 · dish ▾) opens all levels. Below it, pick an AI teammate from two entries: free models (no API key, listed by the server) or a custom model with your own key.
- Results show the income against the goal, the teammate and two big buttons, 再来一次 and 下一关; the next level leads after a win and is also offered after a loss, and on the last level it becomes 全部关卡. Round analysis, all levels and settings sit below as light chips.
- All levels: four per row, the goal, whether it was reached and this browser's best result; a card for player-made levels marks what is coming. The current level is marked by its blue highlight alone.
- Settings is its own card with tabs down the left (AI 搭档, 跨局记忆, 调用上限, 数据与贡献, 声音与语言). The 自定义模型 tile opens just the key form, with a help column.
- Phone menus show the same level plate, partner tiles and result buttons. The language button always reads "English" in Chinese.

### Free models
- Free models now need consent to contribute the round, since the site pays for them: the server refuses a free-model start without it, the card says so, and its decline button opens the own-key form. Own keys and the local version need no contribution.
- Register Gemma 4 26B and Llama 3.1 8B (Cloudflare Workers AI) as later free models. They stay off until their own private key file is installed and the gateway is started with it; each was probed once with a synthetic snapshot only. Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite was registered too, then dropped before it was ever enabled: its free tier allows one call every 4.3 s, while Jeff asks at least every 2 s and right after each step, so nearly every request would have to wait. A key file that still names it keeps working; the field is ignored.
- The Cloudflare models have their own budget below the platform's free tier: 9,000 estimated Neurons a day shared by both. A spent day shows 今日额度已用完 instead of "try again later".
- The free list on every screen follows the server's catalog, so an enabled model appears with no page change.
- Custom models: Gemini moves to 3.5 Flash-Lite (Google no longer opens 2.5 to new accounts). Its note and the model guide say what the free tier means for Jeff: he usually asks more than the 15 requests a minute it allows, so past that he waits before asking again. Cloudflare Workers AI is added with your account ID; online it runs only through the demo server, with its own consent, because Cloudflare refuses browser requests.
- Research records add the service name `cloudflare`. Upgrade the research console before the games, or its records are refused.

### Research records
- Contribution terms move to `pilot-session-2026-11-v8`, so every player is asked again, with a short card: what a round records, and that de-identified data may be published once the research is published. **Details** holds the full terms. New contributions are kept for at most two years, researchers' downloaded copies too. A public dataset would only include rounds contributed at least 30 days before its release, and a round in it can no longer be deleted. Players holding receipts from earlier terms see one more line: agreeing moves those rounds to the new terms with their receipts (`/api/contribution/terms`; the record keeps its `consent_version` and gains `terms`), unless they choose **Keep as they were**. Rounds left under earlier terms keep their expiry. Upgrade the shared data service, both game sites and the research console together.
- Contributed rounds are now kept for six months (180 days) instead of 30 days. The contribution terms move to `pilot-session-2026-10-v7`, so every player is asked again. Rounds saved under the earlier terms keep their 30-day expiry.
- With consent before the round starts, a contributed round can now be replayed exactly: the record keeps the full frozen configuration and every engine input. It also records when each chef actually starts working at a station (`arrival`), whether a throw aims at the other chef (`throw_aim`), what is needed to rebuild every request to Jeff (a digest, recent decisions and the preset messages), the option probabilities a decision model reports, and, with cross-round memory on, which earlier rounds the memory came from. Memory content is still not stored. Jeff's input is unchanged. `scripts/check_research_record.py` checks a record and replays it.
- Contribution storage holds up to 5000 rounds and 5 GB, and refuses saves before the server disk runs low.
- Large research packages download again. The private console now builds a package in the background: a request waits up to 20 seconds, then gets 202 with `Retry-After` until the package is ready, so a build of several minutes (the console runs at 30% of a CPU) no longer holds one connection until a proxy or the network cuts it. A client that leaves does not stop the build, and the finished package waits up to 10 minutes for the same request; before sending, a quick check confirms every round is still active and unchanged. The console page and `scripts/pull_research.py` wait and ask again by themselves.
- `scripts/pull_research.py sync --out DIR` pulls only the rounds DIR does not hold yet, 3 per package by default (`--batch`), with a local list of what came in which package. A local package goes as a whole when it expires, when one of its rounds left the server (withdrawn or expired) or was updated there, as each package's terms require; its other rounds are pulled again. Date filters take a date (`2026-11-03`), not a time, and a wrong one is refused locally.
- `scripts/decision_stats.py` counts a round found in several inputs (overlapping packages) once, from its fullest copy, and reports how many copies it dropped.
- A consented round whose final record could not be written to the database is no longer shown as contributed: the result screen says the contribution failed, and the save is retried once. Before this, such rounds kept only their opening snapshot while the page said they were saved. Wait, continue and stale model answers no longer leave empty action links in event payloads, which had blocked those final saves. Rounds already stored with only an opening snapshot stay as they are, marked incomplete.
- Rounds played with Jeff's plan on keep the switch in `conditions.jeff_plan` (mode, steps, rules version) and, in each `ai_request`, the plan input that rebuilds the request (`jeff_plan`: the plan holding with each step's work and whether it is done, or the work items offered). Rounds with it off have neither field. Private export packages declare `conditions.jeff_plan` in their metadata schema too, so packages with such rounds pass verification; a test keeps the metadata and record schemas in step. Upgrade the research console with this release so it reads them.

### Jeff (the AI teammate)
- Jeff asks for his next step as soon as his current step ends, instead of waiting out the 2-second minimum between requests; the 2 seconds still apply to every other trigger (kitchen changes, urgency bands, the periodic refresh). He also no longer asks in the middle of a step that will end before a typical reply comes back, because that reply was always discarded and, while on its way, held back the question about his next step. In fixed-seed simulations the wait after a step drops to the reply time itself (about 1.2 s instead of 1.8–2.2 s with 1.2 s replies; 3.5 s instead of 5.5–6.0 s with 3.5 s replies), with as many or fewer calls for slower models and up to a third more for very fast ones. `config.json` turns it on with `ai_ask_on_step_end: true`; research records and session records keep it in `request_policy`, so rounds from before and after can be told apart. Jeff's input is unchanged; the payload fingerprints were re-recorded only because the fixed rounds now ask at different moments.
- Two-step intent (trial, off by default; Settings → AI teammate, one plan mode at a time): each time Jeff decides an action he also gives the piece of work that action is for and the one he intends after it; the next request shows them back as a record on that decision in his recent decisions, ticked as he finishes them, not as a plan, and asks again, so each answer replaces the last and there is nothing to keep or drop. The rules version is `rolling-v3`: while food in a pan on a stove is within 8 game seconds of burning, the intent is left out of the request (still asked), because shown to him either as a plan (`rolling-v1`) or as history (`rolling-v2`) it kept him on the intent with a pan about to burn; requests are read by every client as the plan's are. With it on, a change in an order's or a pan's countdown band asks Jeff at once even when his step would end before the reply. `scripts/plan_probe.py --mode rolling` runs the same two emergencies and, in the stale kitchen, how often his new intent repeats the stale one; `scripts/decision_stats.py` counts these rounds as `rolling_rounds`. A sixth payload fingerprint covers level 2 with it on.
- `scripts/plan_probe.py --mode rolling` gives every arm the same previous decision, one that agrees with the kitchen (wait, accepted; Jeff is idle and empty-handed), and only the held arm's carries the intent, so the arms differ only in the intent. The earlier version gave the rolling arms a started fetch he never finished, which pulled him to fetch on its own. `scripts/hands_full_check.py --groups-only` also reports what lay on the stations his walks with full hands went to, alone and with what he held. `scripts/walk_label_check.py --arms as_logged,walks_explained` labels each walk with what lies there and that, with what he holds, no other listed action is there. `--arms as_logged,put_then_take` adds, next to each walk with full hands to a station holding an item, an option to put the held item on a counter (or down where he stands) and then take that item, to see whether he picks it over the walk; the game itself does not offer it.
- How many options Jeff is offered: `scripts/option_count_check.py` (record only, no model call) counts the options of each answered Jev request in the local journals, by the rules version Jeff was sent, and compares what he picked with at most 25 options and with more. `scripts/walk_label_check.py --arms as_logged,no_aimed_throws` sends real walk moments again without the eight aimed throws (throw partner kept), to see whether fewer options change his walks; `--arms` picks the arms, and the default is unchanged.

Whenever Jeff has no plan, its request also asks for the next few pieces of work it intends to do, in order: up to 4 work items of this level (fetch, chop, cook or plate an ingredient, take a clean plate, serve, collect or wash plates), each later step allowed to be none. A step is ticked when Jeff finishes an action doing that work, in any order. The plan has no time limit and no part of the work: it ends when every step is done, when Jeff answers drop to the small keep-or-drop question each request carries while a plan holds, or when the human sends a new preference (messages 1–5), and the request for a new plan then goes out at once, as after a finished step. A missing or unknown answer means no plan (or keeps the plan), and the action still counts. ### Kitchen and controls
- As in Overcooked 2, a board is a counter that also chops, and an ingredient source holds one object on top. Anything that fits on a counter, plated dishes and pans included, can rest on a board or a source, with every counter action there; only a raw ingredient that needs chopping is chopped. While something rests on a source, Space takes that object and the source gives no new ingredient until its top is clear; with full hands, Space at an empty source puts the held item on it. A thrown ingredient can come down on an empty source too. Jeff gets the same actions (put, take, plate, assemble, merge, load at boards and sources) and his rules move to `rules-v9` with sentences saying so.
- Chopping and washing need the hands over the station: the feet within half a cell of the centre line of one of its sides (after the short slide onto it) and at most half a cell behind the usual spot there. Facing a board from the next cell along the counter or diagonally no longer chops thin air; Space says 站到案板正前方才能切. As in Overcooked 2, both chefs can chop one board or wash at one sink together, side by side on a one-sided board too, and progress adds up; Jeff can join that way and, when the other chef stands in front, works beside them. Jeff's rules move to `rules-v8` with one sentence saying so.
- Your own chef keeps the direction you last steered when you stop, as in Overcooked. It used to snap back to the server's older facing for a moment, most visibly after short moves online.
- Extinguishing as in Overcooked 2, for both chefs: spraying lowers the fire's strength, which stays on the station when you walk away, so a few short sprays add up to the 4 seconds; 2 seconds after spraying stops the fire grows back to full over 5 seconds. A red bar on the station shows what is left. The direction held when spraying starts no longer interrupts it. Ruleset: `fire.recovery_game_ms`.
- Throw stays enabled while held, so a phone can always release an aim; throws the server refuses are journaled.
- A second chef can join chopping or washing from any other free side, facing the first chef too, not only from a side at right angles: level 1's middle board can now be shared. Same for both chefs.
- READMEs: a Play online section (https://chefjeff.online/) and a touch controls table next to the keyboard one.
- Online play moves to https://chefjeff.online/ (www too); the first address, chefjeff.belchingleo.online, redirects there. The update script moves the service to the new address once and puts it back if the update fails; certificate renewals of either address reload Nginx.
- As in Overcooked 2, fire stops a board or counter being used but not its surface: what rests there can still be taken (an extinguisher put down on a counter that catches fire is no longer lost), items can be put or plated there and a throw can land there. The same holds for a burning stove: a pan can be lifted off it, put back or filled when empty, but it does not heat until the fire is out, after which food that is not burnt cooks on. Chopping on a burning board and fetching from a burning source still wait for the fire to go out. Items on a burning station stay drawn under the flames.
- Fix: a throw that came down on a board or counter whose centre lay just beyond reach was offered and then refused ("the throw target changed"). This was about 2–3% of the throws offered on levels 1–3, for the player and Jeff alike. Offering and starting a throw now use one reach test, so a throw that ends inside a station's square rests on it.
- Fix: a dish served when no order waits for it (−¥20, either chef) now pops 送错 −¥20 in red over that serving window and plays the failed-serve sound. Before, only the income changed, because the sound was listed under an event name the kitchen no longer sends. A burnt dish the customer refuses (no pay, the order keeps waiting) pops 拒收 in red there with the same sound; before, nothing on screen showed it.

### Level calibration
- The scripted reference chefs behind calibration, the baseline ladder and map audits read what each dish needs from the level (its dishes, and what its vessels cook from which state) instead of knowing only steak and burger. Any recipe in the catalog can now be calibrated, including a dish that needs the pot and the pan at once and one with nothing to cook. Levels 1–3 keep every decision: the 21-seed calibration and the behaviour fingerprints match the recorded ones.
- Fire spread no longer looks up every station's neighbours again on every tick. The layout is fixed for the round, so they are found once. Results are identical and rounds run about five times faster: level 3's calibration takes 54 s instead of 268 s on four processes.
- `level_calibration.py` measures a level a player built, on the release's rules: one order every 30 s, each dish waiting 5 times its chop and cook seconds (60–135 s; levels 1 and 3 come out exactly as authored), and a target of half the scripted pair's median income over 7 order seeds. Prices outside 10–150 and dishes the map cannot make are refused before any round runs; a level the pair serves nothing on is not playable, and one a single chef can pass carries a note. The scripted chefs move to the repository root so the server release carries them.
- A player's level is named by its content (`player-` and 12 hex digits on its level, map, menu and dishes), so it never shares an id with an authored level or another design. Usage statistics and research records count every player-built level as level 0; which design it was stays in the record's resolved configuration. A region on an older release may still report levels 1–3 only, and the research console labels level 0 "玩家关卡".
- Players' levels can be submitted for calibration: `POST /api/player-level/submit` checks a design at once and queues it, `GET /api/player-level/status` reports the queue position, then the pacing and target. One calibration runs at a time in its own low-priority process, one job per page, at most 8 waiting, stopped after 300 s; a design seen before is answered from memory. The local server offers this; the hosted server only with `--player-levels`. See docs/architecture/player-levels.md.
- A calibrated player level can be played: `POST /api/player-level/play` switches the session to it between rounds, like a level change; a restart keeps it and `/api/level` returns to a listed level. Jeff never reads a player's text: the dishes become `dish_1`, `dish_2`… named by their components in English ("bread + chopped lettuce + cooked chicken"), and the player's own names are kept apart for the page. A recipe name may now be up to 160 characters, for those component names. The calibration messages are translated for the English page.
- The level editor's design becomes a level on the server (`level_design.py`): room size, walls, stations and dishes turn into a map, a starting inventory (extinguisher, three plates, each stove's pan or pot) and recipes, so the page sends a small design and every check lives in one place. It refuses walls that cut the room in two, other than exactly one sink, plate return and extinguisher rack, more than two serving windows, fewer than three counters and stations with no floor in front. `build_map` takes walls inside the room.
- Counters may now split a kitchen in two; walls still may not. The map check requires every non-wall cell to be connected and allows the floor in one or two parts. On a split floor one chef spawns in each part, each is offered only the work it can reach, routes keep to its part, and the configuration check requires the parts to be within throw reach. The scripted zoned pair serves from its own side, passes clean plates across and may pass food onto the other side's floor, so such kitchens calibrate. The serve hint points at the nearest serving window the chef can reach, for levels with two. Kitchens in one piece, levels 1–3 included, keep every decision.
- In a player's level the serving windows and the plate return stand against the outer wall, facing into the room: food leaves and dirty plates come back through the wall. The outer wall itself is wall and holds nothing, as in levels 1–3. The page draws every serving window by its type against any of the four walls, its arrows pointing into the wall. The money a dish earns pops up over the window it went out at, no longer always over the first one. On a computer, a room of another size than 14×9 now scales to fit the board and is centred (a 16×10 room's bottom wall used to cover the status lines; phones already fitted every size). Levels 1–3 look and play as before.
- A player's dishes show on the page under the player's own names, as written in either language, on the tickets, the hand and the plate. A dish made of exactly an official dish's components borrows its picture (红烧牛肉面 is drawn as the beef noodles), sent as `player_level.looks`; any other finished dish is drawn as its ingredients' layers on the plate, bread split into the burger's bun, instead of a placeholder. Jeff's input and the game's rules do not change, and levels 1–3 look as before.
- The engine's lines on the page (a new order, a dish nobody ordered, hints) also say a player's dish names, on a computer and a phone, instead of the English components Jeff reads; the state the server sends Jeff is unchanged.
- The level editor opens from the "+ 自定义关卡" card where the server offers player levels. My levels keeps a player's levels in this browser, with export to and import from a file. The editor has three steps. The kitchen layout picks the room size, places stations and walls by click or drag, removes a station or wall clicked again or erased in a stroke, and explains where a serving window or plate return goes. The menu sets each dish's name, ingredients and price with the suggested price, the English name Jeff reads and the customers' patience. It offers only the ingredients the kitchen layout can make: each needs its box, a board if it is chopped and the right stove if it is cooked. The rest are greyed out with what each lacks, and boxes no dish uses are listed. Calibrate and play submits the design, follows its place in the queue, shows the target and whether one chef alone passes, and switches the kitchen to the level. ### 厨房与操作
- 和《胡闹厨房 2》一样，案板就是能切菜的柜台，食材箱顶上也能放一件东西。柜台上能放的东西，包括装好菜的盘子和锅，都能放在案板或食材箱上，柜台上的操作在那里也都能做；只有需要切的生食材才会被切。食材箱上放着东西时，按空格拿起的是那件东西，要等顶上空了才能再取料；手里拿着东西对着空食材箱按空格，会把东西放上去。扔出去的食材也能落在空食材箱上。Jeff 有同样的动作（在案板和食材箱上放下、拿起、装盘、加料、合盘、入锅），规则升到 `rules-v9`，加了几句说明。
- 切菜和洗碗时手要在工位正上方：脚离某一侧的中线不超过半格（按侧移到中线之后的位置算），最多比平时的站位往后半格。从台面上隔壁一格或斜对角对着案板，不会再对着空气切菜，按空格会提示“站到案板正前方才能切”。和《胡闹厨房 2》一样，两位厨师可以一起切同一块案板、一起洗同一个水槽，单面案板也能并排，进度相加；Jeff 也能这样加入，对方站在正前方时它会站到旁边。Jeff 的规则升到 `rules-v8`，多了一句说明这件事。
- 松开方向键时，自己的厨师保持最后按的方向，和《胡闹厨房》一样。以前会先转回服务器那边还没更新的旧朝向，再转回来，线上短距离挪位置时最明显。
- 灭火改成和《胡闹厨房 2》一样，两位厨师规则相同：喷会降低火势，走开后进度保留在工位上，几次短喷可以累计到 4 秒；停喷 2 秒后火会在 5 秒内长回满。工位上的红色条显示剩下多少。开始喷时摇杆还按着的方向不会打断灭火。规则集新增 `fire.recovery_game_ms`。
- 按住「扔」时按钮不会被禁用，手机上总能松手结束瞄准；服务器拒绝的扔会留下记录。
- 第二个人可以从任意另一侧空位加入一起切菜、洗碗，包括站在第一个人对面，不再只限于和他成直角的一侧：第一关中间的 2 号案板现在也能两人一起切。两位厨师规则相同。
- README 新增「在线试玩」（https://chefjeff.online/），触屏操作单独列表，和键盘操作并列。
- 在线试玩改到 https://chefjeff.online/（带 www 也可以），原来的地址 chefjeff.belchingleo.online 会跳转过去。更新脚本会一次性把服务改到新地址，更新失败则改回去；两个地址的证书续期后都会重新加载 Nginx。
- 和《胡闹厨房 2》一样，火只让柜台、案板不能“使用”，不影响台面：上面的东西照样能拿走（放在柜台上的灭火器遇火后不会再被困住），也能往上放、装盘，扔过去也能落在上面。着火的灶台也一样：锅可以端走、放回去，空锅还能加料，但灭火前不加热，灭火后没糊的食物接着加热。在着火的案板上切菜、从着火的取料处取料，仍要先灭火。着火工位上的东西会画在火焰下面，不再消失。
- 修复：扔的落点如果是一块中心略超出射程的案板或柜台，这个扔法会被列出来，按下去却被拒绝，提示“抛递目标已变化”。前三关列出的扔法里约有 2% 到 3% 属于这种情况，玩家和 Jeff 都会遇到。现在列出和执行用同一个射程判断，扔到工位方格以内就落在上面。

### 关卡校准
- 校准、基线阶梯和地图检查用的脚本厨师，改为从关卡数据里读每道菜要什么、每口锅能把什么从哪一步煮熟，不再只会做牛排和汉堡。所以菜谱目录里的任何一道菜都能校准，包括同时要用汤锅和平底锅的菜，以及完全不用下锅的菜。前三关的每一步决定都没有变，21 个种子的校准结果和行为指纹都和原来一致。
- 引擎判断火会不会蔓延时，原来每一帧都要重新找一遍每个工位挨着谁。因为一局里地图不会变，现在只找一次。结果完全相同，一局快了约五倍：第三关校准在 4 个进程下从 268 秒降到 54 秒。
- 新增 `level_calibration.py`，按发布版的规则给玩家做的关卡算订单节奏和目标：每 30 秒来一单，顾客耐心是这道菜切和煮总时间的 5 倍（60–135 秒，第一关和第三关算出来和原来完全一样），目标是脚本双人在 7 个订单种子上收入中位数的一半。价格不在 10–150 之间、地图做不出来的菜，会在开跑前被拦下；脚本双人一道菜都做不出的关卡不能玩，一个人也能过的关卡会加提示。脚本厨师挪到仓库根目录，跟着服务器一起发布。
- 玩家关卡按内容生成编号（关卡、地图、菜单和菜都用 `player-` 加 12 位十六进制），不会和官方关卡或别的设计撞号。在线统计和研究记录把所有玩家关卡记作第 0 关，具体是哪一张，研究记录里存的完整配置写得清楚。还在旧版本上的地区服务器可以继续只报第 1–3 关；研究后台把第 0 关显示为“玩家关卡”。
- 玩家关卡可以提交校准了：`POST /api/player-level/submit` 当场检查设计并排进队列，`GET /api/player-level/status` 返回排队位置，算完后返回订单节奏和目标。一次只校准一张，在单独的低优先级进程里跑；每个页面同时只能排一张，最多 8 张等候，超过 300 秒自动停止；算过的设计直接用之前的结果。本地服务器默认提供；线上服务器要加 `--player-levels` 才打开。说明见 docs/architecture/player-levels.md。
- 校准好的玩家关卡可以开局了：`POST /api/player-level/play` 在两局之间把当前厨房换成这张关卡，用法和换关一样；重开一局仍是这张关卡，用 `/api/level` 换回官方关卡。Jeff 读不到玩家写的任何文字：玩家的菜改成 `dish_1`、`dish_2`……名字是英文配料组合（“bread + chopped lettuce + cooked chicken”），玩家自己起的菜名单独存放，只给页面显示。为了放下这些配料名，菜名长度上限放宽到 160 个字符。校准相关的提示都加了英文翻译。
- 关卡编辑器的设计稿在服务器上变成完整关卡（`level_design.py`）：房间尺寸、墙、设备和菜，生成地图、开局物品（灭火器、3 个盘子、每个灶台配好的平底锅或汤锅）和菜谱。网页只需要交一份简单的设计稿，所有检查都在服务器这一处。以下情况会被拦下：墙把房间隔断；水槽、脏盘回收、灭火器架不是正好各 1 个；出餐口超过 2 个；柜台少于 3 个；设备跟前没有地方站。`build_map` 支持在房间里面画墙。

## 0.6.0-beta.1 — 2026-10-01 · 首次公开测试版

游戏改为运行在数据驱动内核上：每一关都由五个数据模型（地图、菜谱、订单、设备、关卡）描述，引擎里没有针对某一关的代码。

### 规则
- 所有关卡使用同一套规则：每局 180 游戏秒，目标是关店时的净收入。订单持续到关店，关店时仍未完成的订单不扣钱。
- 糊菜：糊了 5 秒以内的按价格减 10 元收下；糊得更久会被拒收，订单继续等待。送出没有订单在等的菜扣 20 元。
- 删除 0.5.9 的旧规则：差评、出餐数目标、达标立即结束、剩余时间奖励。
- 第一关是练习关；第二、三关按参考双人组校准。

### 厨房与操作
- 胡闹厨房式键盘操作，按键避开浏览器冲突：空格对面前的东西操作（按住可瞄准投掷），Shift 冲刺，Enter 标记。
- 牛肉用平底锅煎；引擎也支持煮面条的汤锅，一个灶台同时只放一口锅。
- 盘子、菜、平底锅和灭火器可在 4 格内传递；食材可在 4 格内抛出。
- 盘子或食材放在地上时，也能像在柜台上一样组装。
- 厨师对所有工位使用同一身体尺寸：不再踩进柜台正面，走到哪里停下就在哪里操作。
- 行走时能滑过浅凹口；卡住的路线会绕开对方重新规划；“走向搭档”可以主动走到对方身边。

### Jeff（AI 队友）
- Jeff 的输入里厨师称呼明确（`human`、`jeff`），重复选项更少；盘子缺的部件写明所需状态。
- Jeff 看到的规则和引擎来自同一份冻结配置，不含任何“应该合作”的价值措辞。

### 记录与分析
- 每局记录为一条事件流，并生成可回放的对局包。
- 关店后显示本局记录：谁做了哪些活。
- 物品来历追踪与协作分析。本局记录显示三项试验性指标：按标准时间计算的贡献（配置里的操作时间加必要的最短走路）、超出理想的拖延（在两人之间分摊）和空转时间。分析工具还会给出每道菜的关键路径。
- 容量分析器，以及校准阶梯（单人、单人加随机搭档、双人）。
- 三关的行为指纹，防止规则被无意改动。

### 画面与声音
- 厨房界面设计规范、像素字体、音乐与音效。
- 两名厨师的每个视角都从同一个身体母版画出，切菜时不再变大；刀单独一层。
- 新增八种食材（黄瓜、洋葱、芝士、葱、鸡肉、鱼、饼、面条）和六道菜谱及像素图，可用于新关卡。
- 糊了的菜在任何位置都能认出来。
